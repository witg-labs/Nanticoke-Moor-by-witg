# Book Catalog Allocation Map

Status: PROPOSAL ONLY. Prepared 2026-10-27 and submitted for review in this proposal PR at the direction of the archive owner ("Work on tier A"). Merging this PR adds only these staging documents to the repo. It does NOT ingest any fact into the archive database (preservation_output/genealogy_preservation.db), does NOT change any generated person export, and does NOT change the live archive. Database ingestion and re-export require a separate go-ahead.

Purpose: allocate the person-named facts from the four verified book fact catalogs (Forgotten Folk; Speck 1915; Weslager 1948; Norwood 2007) to archive profiles, to new-profile proposals, or to holds, following the same staging pattern as ARCHIVE_ENRICHMENT_MAP.md and archive_enrichment_ingestion.json. The hard architecture rule from that map governs here too: the per-person JSON files and persons_summary.json are generated exports from preservation_output/genealogy_preservation.db and are never hand-edited. Facts are staged as JSON for ingestion, then exported.

Tier A entries are staged in book_catalog_ingestion.json. Everything else in this map is a hold, a new-profile proposal, or an ambiguity record. Nothing in Tiers B, C, or D is staged for ingestion.

## Method and screening accounting

All four catalogs were screened in full by delegated screening passes (no sampling), then every Tier A and Tier D finalist was re-checked by the coordinator against the person's detail file in the archive checkout (frontend/public/api/person/{id}.json). Detail-file checks were scripted full reads of each candidate's facts and relationships; fact values were read in full for all Tier A finalists and for every conflation-critical Tier D profile. Person matching was against persons_summary.json (3,789 entries), exact name first, then name variants (Harmon/Harman, Sockum/Sockume, Hansor/Hanzer/Hanzar/Hansley/Handsor, Clark/Clarke). Surname alone was never treated as a match.

## Standing rules applied

- Tier A requires all of: a full page citation in the book; a specific identifiable person; exactly one matching archive profile by name AND by date, place, or family consistency; the fact not already on that profile in substance; and the fact outside every exclusion class.
- The 1881 Incorporated Body list is a group fact (Tier B as a group). Individual names from it were staged in Tier A only after a per-name check: the name appears in both the Forgotten Folk transcription (printed p. 115 / PDF p. 137) and the Norwood 2007 booklet transcription (printed p. 39) of the act list, and claim audit item D12 verified the act itself (16 Del. Laws ch. 359). Names failing uniqueness or date checks stayed in Tier D or Tier C.
- [OCR uncertain] facts are never Tier A. Author interpretations and assessments are never Tier A. Hedges travel with the fact; no hedged statement was upgraded, and no Tier A entry is hedged.
- Photo facts allocate to a person_id, never to a photo_id. Archive photo_ids (Nicey Harmon 1169, Stephen Sockum 2282, Clem Carney 202, Robert Coker 3234, Speck Plates 3662 and 3663) are recorded as photo evidence only. Speck's Plates II to XI, XIV, XVI, XVII, and XX name no individuals, and the Weslager 1948 community plates name none either; no allocation was guessed from them.
- Facts already staged in archive_enrichment_ingestion.json are marked OVERLAP and are not double-staged; a book fact adding something new to those persons may be Tier A with the overlap noted.
- Carried-in decisions applied without re-litigation: Lydia facts stage to canonical 9334 with the merge dependency noted, and 7577 gets none; the Nicey Harmon book facts carry the caveat that the book names her only "Nicey Harmon" and does not confirm the archive fuller name or the year 1830; W. R. Clark allocates only on a unique dates-and-family fit, which did not occur; a trail running only through Mitsawokett is Tier B at best; no 17th or 18th century person is attached to a later namesake.
